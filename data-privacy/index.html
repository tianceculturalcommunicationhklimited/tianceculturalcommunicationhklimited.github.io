Lowdeck Privacy Policy

Effective: 28 October 2026
Seller: TIANCE CULTURAL COMMUNICATION (HK) LIMITED, Hong Kong
Privacy contact: contact@tianceculturalcommunicationhklimited.com

Lowdeck is a golf card game for iPhone in which you play against computer rivals. On this page, "the seller" means TIANCE CULTURAL COMMUNICATION (HK) LIMITED, the party shown as seller of Lowdeck on the App Store. The sections below cover what the game writes to your iPhone, how to clear it, and the single situation in which personal data reaches the seller.

In three sentences

There is no sign-up in Lowdeck, and the game has no way to go online: no part of it opens an internet link. The seller learns nothing about how, when or how often you play, because the game contains no component for ads, usage analytics, crash reports or tracking. Personal data can reach the seller one way only: in an email you decide to send.

What Lowdeck writes to your iPhone

Everything listed here is kept in a private folder that iOS assigns to Lowdeck. Other apps cannot read it, and none of it is copied to the seller.

  • The game in progress. One file, rewritten after every move by you or a rival. It holds the table setup (6 or 9 cards, how many rivals, their level, how many holes), the position of every card, including face-down cards and the order of the draw pile, the card someone is holding, the points of each finished hole and the last few moves shown on the table. All shuffles of a game derive from one number that iOS picks at random when the game is dealt; that number is unrelated to you and to your iPhone.
  • Your record. For each rival level, games played and games won; for each grid size, holes played, total points and the best hole. So that no result is counted twice, the file also remembers the random number of the last game counted and the last hole counted in it.
  • Preferences. Sound, Haptics, Animation speed, Appearance, Confirm with a second tap, the table setup you chose last, and whether the introduction was completed or skipped.
  • Files put aside. A saved game or record that cannot be opened, or that a newer version of Lowdeck wrote, is renamed and left where it is instead of being deleted or overwritten.
  • Diagnostic lines. When a file cannot be written or has to be put aside, Lowdeck writes a short technical line, such as "game write failed", into the device's system log. No cards, scores or names appear in it, and Lowdeck never sends the log anywhere.

Erasing it. End game in the table menu erases the saved game; so does leaving the final scorecard with Play again or Home. Reset record in Preferences returns every count to zero. Removing Lowdeck from your iPhone deletes all of the above, put-aside files included.

Backup copies. Backups of your iPhone, whether kept in iCloud or on a computer, may include these files. Apple stores such copies for you, or your computer does; either way you manage them, and the seller cannot read them.

What Lowdeck leaves alone

The game asks iOS for no permission of any kind. It has no use for your contacts, camera, health data, calendars, location or photo library. It plays three short sounds that are calculated inside the app and never records or listens through the microphone. No notifications are sent, and because nothing is tracked, the iOS tracking request never comes up. Lowdeck displays no web pages of its own either: the Privacy and Support entries in Preferences hand their address to your browser.

Email to the seller

Lowdeck does not send email itself. If you write to contact@tianceculturalcommunicationhklimited.com, the seller receives your email address and display name as your mail service sends them, the message text and any attachments. These are used to answer you and, when you report a fault, to reproduce and correct it. They are not used for marketing, not sold and not handed to anyone else. An email provider runs the mailbox for the seller. Each message is deleted once the exchange is over, or before that if you ask.

If you live in the EEA, Britain or Switzerland, the legal ground for this is the seller's legitimate interest in replying to messages sent to it, under point (f) of Article 6(1) GDPR and the corresponding UK and Swiss provisions.

Figures that come from Apple

The seller can see overall totals in App Store Connect, for example the number of downloads per country. Apple can also pass along crash reports and usage statistics, but only from people who have turned that on among the iPhone's privacy settings (Analytics & Improvements, then Share With App Developers). Apple operates that collection and its own privacy policy covers it; the reports arrive without your name or contact details, and the seller looks at them solely to track down faults. Turning the same switch off stops it.

Children

Lowdeck is a card game scored in points. It has no chat, no contact between players, no purchases and nothing that can be staked or collected, so people of every age can play it. The seller does not knowingly gather personal data from children. Should a child have written to the seller, a parent may ask for the message to be erased.

Your rights

Your country's law may grant you rights over personal data: the GDPR and its UK and Swiss equivalents do, and so does California's Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA). Typically you may see the personal data a business has about you, request a duplicate, have it put right or removed, oppose or restrict its use, and take a complaint to the regulator in charge of data protection. With Lowdeck, the only personal data the seller can hold is email you sent. Write to contact@tianceculturalcommunicationhklimited.com and you will receive an answer within the deadline set by the law that applies to you. No personal data is sold or shared, as the CCPA uses those words.

Changes
